The tag says what the content means,
not what it looks like.

Same as last class. Headings were an outline, not sizes. Emphasis is meaning, not bold. Every time, the visual result is identical and one version carries meaning.

Two pairs, one rule
<strong>
This is important. Use it.
<em>
Say this with stress. Use it.
<b> <i>
Just bold. Just italic. No meaning at all.

When both work, pick the one that means something.

Today's learning target

I can use semantic formatting to mark up meaning,
and comments to label and debug my page.

Students will demonstrate this learning target with at least 80% accuracy on today's checkpoint.

Your page will look almost exactly the same at the end of today. That is not a bug. What changed is that a machine can now tell which parts matter.
Today's deliverables
  1. Real emphasis — at least two <strong> or <em> that mark meaning, not decoration
  2. A quote — one <blockquote> with a <cite>, from a real source about your topic
  3. Comments labelling each section of your page
  4. Still works — one <h1>, everything closed, renders clean
  5. Committed and pushed to your my-site repo

Step 1 · 22 minutes

Mark up real emphasis

  • Read your own paragraphs from last class
  • Find the phrases that genuinely matter — wrap those in <strong>
  • Find a phrase you'd say differently out loud — wrap that in <em>
  • edit → save → refresh
Two or three per page is plenty. Not two or three per sentence.
You have to read
your own writing
to do this.

That's the assignment. Nobody can tell you which of your sentences is the important one.

Step 2 · 15 minutes

Quote a source

  1. Find a real quote about your topic — one or two sentences
  2. Wrap it in <blockquote>
  3. Name the source underneath in <cite>
<blockquote> is a block — it stands apart from your paragraphs on its own. That's the browser telling you it's someone else's words.
Step 3 · 12 minutes

Comments do
two different jobs

  • Notes. Label your sections so future-you can find things. Leave yourself a TODO.
  • A switch. Anything inside a comment disappears from the page without being deleted.
You already know this move from Python. Same idea, different symbols.
Step 4 · Break it on purpose · 8 minutes

Comment out
a whole section.

Save. Refresh. Watch it disappear. Then put it back.

Turn things off until it works. Then turn the last one back on.

That's how you find what's broken — in HTML, in Python, in anything.

If it's not working

An unclosed thing swallows the rest of the page. Same as last class's missing </h1>.

Finished early?
